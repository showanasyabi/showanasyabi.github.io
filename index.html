About

I'm a Senior Member of Technical Staff at Oracle, working on the IO path of the cloud-scale block storage behind Exadata. My day-to-day begins with a hard performance problem (memory efficiency, throughput, tail latency) and ends at its root cause. Sometimes fixing it takes a careful patch; sometimes it takes rethinking how we do I/O altogether.

I completed my Ph.D. in Computer Science at Boston University in 2023, advised by Professor Azer Bestavros, with a thesis on resource-efficient, performant in-memory key-value stores for at-scale datacenters.

I've also grown interested in the AI stack: inference routing and the resource efficiency of GPUs and memory. It's familiar territory in new clothes: KV-cache management, request scheduling under latency SLOs, and memory under pressure are the same problems I've spent the past decade on.

Interests: AI-stack resource efficiency (inference routing, GPUs, memory) · key-value stores & caching · operating systems & scheduling · stream processing

When I'm not working, I'm traveling, chasing down Boston's best tables, or playing every sport there is. Pro at none, enthusiastic at all.

Research

In-Memory Key-Value Stores & Caching

The core of my Ph.D.: the throughput, scalability, and resource efficiency of in-memory key-value stores (see my survey). Existing power-saving techniques break down under the microsecond service times and high arrival rates these systems face, so I moved CPU scheduling out of the operating system and into the application, where domain knowledge lives. The result, Peafowl (SoCC '20), reduces power consumption while preserving microsecond-scale tail latency. At Twitter, I analyzed the memory efficiency of timeline caches under high write throughput; the findings fed directly into my thesis.

Operating Systems & Clouds

Designed and built several Xen hypervisor schedulers improving virtualized clouds' tail latency (TerrierTail), predictability (ppXen), utilization (Akita), and quality of service (Kani). Also redesigned the Linux CFS scheduler and menu governor to improve datacenter latency (CTS) and power consumption (Yawn). This thread continues in my work at Oracle, on the hot I/O path of a cloud-scale storage driver.

Stream Processing Systems

Improved stream processing throughput by understanding state workloads and designing key-value stores tailored to them. I carried out an empirical characterization of state workloads by instrumenting Apache Flink and RocksDB, then built Gadget (EuroSys '22), a benchmark harness for robust, systematic evaluation of streaming state stores.

Experience

Mar 2023 – Present

Senior Member of Technical Staff · Oracle, Exadata/Exascale Storage

Design and optimization of the I/O path of Oracle's cloud-scale block storage: a Linux block driver bridging the kernel block layer with the distributed storage backend behind Oracle's cloud database offerings.

  • Increased our driver throughput by 55% with Parallel Lock, a low-contention locking mechanism that replaced traditional locks on the hot IO path and removed a key scalability bottleneck.
  • Cut memory usage by 35% by restructuring the IO path to shrink per-IO descriptors, with no loss of performance or observability.
  • Raised RDMA read coverage to ~95% by diagnosing and fixing defects across the RDMA I/O path, improving both latency and throughput.
  • Reduced tail latency of the elastic memory subsystem under dynamic workloads by eliminating cold-start behavior.
